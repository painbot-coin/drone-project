Abstract
Unmanned aerial vehicles have become essential tools in search and rescue operations
due to their capability to safely access hazardous zones while providing real-time situational
awareness. Deploying drones in complex urban environments requires advanced path planning
systems that can optimize ﬂight routes while responding to dynamic environmental conditions.
The A* algorithm and reinforcement learning (RL) based approaches have been proposed
for drone path planning and achieved promising results. However, the A* algorithm fails
to adapt when environmental conditions transform during mission operations. The training
process of pure RL-based methods needs long periods of time to produce reliable results while
using many samples. Moreover, the unpredictable movements of dynamic obstacles require
real-time response, making it even more diﬃcult for existing approach.
To address the above problems, in this dissertation, we propose a hybrid framework for
eﬃcient and real-time path planning. The hybrid framework combines A* and reinforcement
learning, where A* generates the initial path plan and RL optimize the plan to adapt to dynamic
environment. To optimize the eﬃciency, we further propose Experience Replay Buﬀer which
saves previous experiences for future learning through random batch selection to eliminate
time-based dependencies and produce multiple learning opportunities from each experience.
Even with the hybrid framework, the successful rate of path planning is still limited.
To further enhance path planning eﬃciency and eﬀectiveness, we introduce a Multi-Level
RL Policies through a three-agent hierarchical structure which uses Main RL for strategic
heuristic guidance, Local RL for instant obstacle clearance and Global RL for essential route
optimization. RL-Based Obstacle Prediction is to forecast upcoming obstacle locations based
on movement patterns in pattern-based dynamic environments.
We evaluated the proposed approach in a 2D grid simulation environment, and compare
with SOTA approach D*. Evaluation results show the algorithm achieves 100% success in static
environments through its ability to generate optimal paths. The system achieves 93.3% success
rate when operating in environments with moving obstacles whereas A* and D* Lite achieve
30.0% and 73.3% success rates respectively. The obstacle prediction method in pattern-based

highest energy conservation rate of 15.1% when battery reserves drop to their lowest point. The
system achieves a 41.2% reduction in training time through its application of transfer learning
methods. The system achieves successful global optimization through real-time local adaptation
which creates an operational system for autonomous search and rescue operations that provides
quick response times and adaptable mission performance.

1.2

Background
Drones have become transformative tools in various civilian and emergency response

applications. Their capabilities enormously enhance the eﬃciency of operations, demonstrating
their potential to contribute positively to society. These autonomous or remotely piloted
aircraft systems oﬀer mobility, ﬂexibility [1], and aerial perspective capabilities, making them
particularly valuable in time-critical scenarios such as search and rescue (SAR) operations.
The integration of drones in SAR operations has shown remarkable potential [1–3] to enhance
the eﬃciency and eﬀectiveness of emergency response eﬀorts, especially in scenarios where
traditional ground-based search methods face signiﬁcant limitations or risks that the drones
cannot be aﬀected by.

Search and rescue missions are challenging and time-sensitive missions aimed at ﬁnding
and rescuing people in distress or imminent danger. The conventional approach to SAR
operations, which primarily relies on ground teams and human-crewed aircraft, often faces
challenges such as limited visibility, diﬃcult terrain accessibility, and signiﬁcant resource
requirements regarding personnel and equipment.
Through their unique characteristics, drones present a compelling solution to many
challenges. Their ability to rapidly deploy, operate in challenging environments, provide
real-time aerial surveillance, and operate in a timely manner makes them invaluable assets
in SAR missions. Moreover, the reasonably low operational costs and reduced risk to
human personnel make drones attractive for emergency response organizations. However,
eﬀectively utilizing drones in SAR operations requires educated path planning and optimization
capabilities [4] to maximize their potential impact.
Path planning, in the context of drone operations, refers to locating and determining the
optimal route for a drone to navigate through from its current position to a target location while
considering various constraints and objectives. These constraints typically include obstacle
avoidance, battery limitations, and environmental conditions which aﬀect the mission's success.
In SAR scenarios, eﬀective path planning becomes particularly crucial as it directly impacts the
probability of successfully locating the operations targets in the shortest possible time while
managing limited resources eﬃciently [5].
The complexity of drone path planning and optimization in SAR missions is heightened
by several factors[6]:
1. Real time Decision making: The need to process and respond to dynamic environmental
information [7, 8] and changing mission parameters in real-time
2. Resource optimization and balancing: managing limited battery life while maximizing
search coverage
3. Environmental challenges: navigating through complex urban or natural environments
with various obstacles.
4. Target uncertainty: operating with incomplete or uncertain information about the target
precise location.

1.4

Key Research Problems
The research solves several critical issues in autonomous drone path planning for search

and rescue missions through the implementation of a Path optimization A* and reinforcement
learning system.
1. Dynamic Obstacle Navigation in Real-Time The research addresses the critical
challenge of navigating through shifting obstacles through its integration of A*
pathﬁnding with an improved reinforcement learning system. The A* algorithm which
4

北京航空航天大学硕士学位论文

exists in its traditional form operates only in environments without movement because
it fails to detect the moving obstacles which search and rescue missions encounter. The
Hybrid system [20] implements three separate solutions to handle this issue: A* real-time
replanning becomes active when obstacles appear in the planned route which results in
a fast response time and RL learning operates throughout execution to update Q-values
based on drone movements for better adaptation to changing situations and RL-based
obstacle prediction which monitors movement patterns to predict upcoming obstacle
locations for early avoidance. The system uses multiple instance RL agents which operate
with two distinct policies for global and local control—the Local RL agent performs
immediate obstacle avoidance with quick responses, and the Global RL agent handles
signiﬁcant path modiﬁcations when local adjustments prove inadequate. The system uses
this hierarchical method to reach high success rate in changing environments which
results in a better performance than basic A*[21].
2. The Need for Optimality and Adaptability The research develops a hybrid system
which solves the main problem of discovering the best routes while preserving
environmental adaptability. The A* algorithm produces the most eﬃcient routes [22, 23]
but it becomes unable to ﬁnd new paths when moving obstacles start blocking its
originally planned route. The ﬂexibility of pure reinforcement learning methods exists
but these methods fail to reach their best performance while needing long periods of
training. The Hybrid system solves this conﬂict by using RL to guide heuristic learning
which enables Q-values to modify A* search heuristics through a continuous exchange
of knowledge between classical and learning-based planning methods. The system uses
RL-based path reﬁnement to enhance A* paths through learned preference application
which maintains A*’s optimality beneﬁts while RL adapts to new situations. The system
unites A* optimization with RL adaptability to create an entire solution which handles
unpredictable search and rescue scenarios.
3. Ineﬃciency in Pure Reinforcement Learning approach The research addresses
the main issue of sample ineﬃciency in pure reinforcement learning through four
improvement additions to the MR-QLearning algorithm. The Experience Replay

Machine Learning Based Algorithm Systems
Q-Learning [31–33] represents a model-free RL algorithm which teaches action-value

functions Q(s, a) to predict the total reward that results from performing action a in state s and
then using the optimal policy for subsequent decisions[34]. The algorithm performs Q-value
updates through temporal diﬀerence learning which depends on observed rewards together with
future Q-values. The Q-value update follows the Bellman equation[35]:
Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α[r + γ max Q(s′ , a′ ) − Q(s, a)]

(2.1)

The update rule contains three main components which are α for learning rate between 0 and 1
and γ for discount factor between 0 and 1 and r for immediate reward and s′ for next state[36].
The oﬀ-policy algorithm Q-Learning operates through an exploration policy (ε-greedy) which
enables it to discover the best policy[37, 38].
Double Q-Learning [39, 40] solves the overestimation problem which Q-learning
experiences because the max operator in its update process leads to excessive action value
overestimation. The system operates with two separate Q-tables named Q1 and Q2 which serve
for action selection and evaluation assessment. The update process moves between the two
tables:
Q1 (s, a) ← Q1 (s, a) + α[r + γQ2 (s′ , arg max Q1 (s′ , a′ )) − Q1 (s, a)]

(2.2)

The separation of action selection from evaluation processes helps to minimize overestimation
errors which results in more stable learning outcomes. The research shows Double Q-Learning
outperforms Q-Learning in terms of speed and policy reliability when operating in stochastic
environments.

(DQN) architecture from [42] for deep reinforcement learning. The method uses a replay
buﬀer to store previous experiences which include state information and action selection

3.2.1

Heuristic Function Design
The A* implementation uses the Manhattan distance as its base heuristic function which

calculates the total absolute diﬀerence between the current node position and the goal position
through horizontal and vertical distance calculations. The Manhattan distance between a node
located at (x, y) and a goal position at (g x , gy ) equals the sum of the absolute diﬀerences between
x and g x and between y and gy . The heuristic functions as an admissible and consistent solution
for grid-based environments which allow four-directional movement because it calculates the
Manhattan distance which represents the shortest path to the goal when there are no obstacles.
The mathematical properties of this heuristic ensure that A* will always ﬁnd the shortest
path when one exists. The Manhattan distance serves as an eﬀective base heuristic but the
Hybrid system builds upon this foundation through context-based adjustments which modify
the heuristic according to operational requirements. The following subsections explain how
these extensions convert the ﬁxed heuristic into an adaptive evaluation system which adapts to
battery limitations and acquired environmental data.
3.2.2

Battery-Aware Heuristic Enhancements
The A* implementation includes a battery-aware heuristic enhancement which adjusts

path evaluation through the drone’s current energy levels. The system fulﬁlls the essential
operational need for drones to arrive at their targets before their batteries run out while the need
for arrival becomes more critical as the drone’s energy supply decreases. The battery-aware
heuristic uses multiplicative scaling factors which apply to the base Manhattan distance through
discrete battery threshold values. The heuristic functions as usual when battery capacity stays
above ﬁfty percent because it enables the algorithm to select paths based solely on distance
without any modiﬁcations. The search direction shifts toward shorter paths when battery levels
reach below ﬁfty percent through the application of a 1.3 scaling factor to the heuristic.
The system increases its scaling factor to 2.0 when battery capacity reaches thirty percent
or lower to select the most eﬃcient path toward the objective. The system implements a

Multi-Level RL Integration for Path Optimization

The Multi-Level reinforcement learning policies learns to use various planning lengths
with distinct methods. Diﬀerent lengths of planning involve diﬀerent learning techniques.
Strategies requiring rapid responses for immediate avoidance of obstacles, require little
computing power. However, route selection procedures which need to take account of all the
environment require more computational resources and longer planning periods. The proposed
solution combines three distinct Q-learning agents operating at diﬀerent time intervals, these
elements are controlled by a hierarchical decision process.

System Architecture
The Multi-Level RL system employs a hierarchical architecture comprising three

specialized reinforcement learning agents, each optimized for a speciﬁc planning horizon. In
the architecture, resources are allocated to match the computational needs of tasks that require
varying levels of complexity. This is achieved through a system of resources that coordinate
with each other to present a uniﬁed approach.

The architecture of the system consists of three reinforcement learning agents which are

hierarchically structured. In this architecture, agents keep their own individual Q-learning tables
to themselves. Training procedures for the agents are customized in accordance with their roles

Main RL Agent
The Main RL Agent learns the overall patterns of the environment from the system

following extensive learning. With a training budget of 300 episodes, this learning agent is

produce viable paths.
4.7.2

RL-Based Obstacle Prediction Mechanism
The obstacle prediction mechanism represents a distinctive capability of the Multi-Level

RL system that enables proactive avoidance of dynamic obstacles. This mechanism analyzes
historical obstacle movement data to forecast future positions.
Movement Pattern Detection
The prediction system maintains a temporal buﬀer of obstacle positions observed over
recent time steps. Pattern detection operates by analyzing direction vectors between consecutive
obstacle positions. For prediction to be enabled, an obstacle must exhibit consistent movement
over at least three consecutive time steps:
Consistent(o) = ∀i ∈ [1, n − 1] : dir(pi , pi+1 ) = dir(pi+1 , pi+2 )

(4.9)

where o represents an obstacle, pi denotes its position at time step i, and dir(a, b) computes
the unit direction vector from position a to position b.
Position Extrapolation
Once a consistent movement pattern is detected, the system extrapolates future positions
by projecting the obstacle along its established trajectory:

pt+k = pt + k × (dx, dy)

(4.10)

where (dx, dy) represents the consistent movement direction vector and k ranges from 1 to
the lookahead horizon.
Prediction Accuracy
The obstacle prediction mechanism operates eﬀectively only when obstacles exhibit
consistent, predictable movement patterns. For obstacles that move randomly with direction
changes at each time step, prediction cannot be performed.

Analysis of Hierarchical Beneﬁts. The path length improvements observed in this
ablation study can be attributed to several mechanisms enabled by the multi-level architecture:
1. Specialized Agent Roles: The Local RL agent (100 episodes, 50-step horizon) provides
rapid local optimization, while the Global RL agent (150 episodes, 100-step horizon)
handles strategic route selection. This division of labor enables more eﬀective exploration
of the solution space compared to a single agent attempting both tasks.

74

北京航空航天大学硕士学位论文

2. RL-Based Path Reﬁnement: The Local RL agent’s Q-values are used to reﬁne
A*-generated paths, identifying learned shortcuts that reduce overall path length. Without
this component, paths follow purely A*-optimal routes without learned improvements.
3. Cascade Replanning: The hierarchical cascade mechanism (Local → Global → A*
fallback) enables graduated responses to obstacles, with simpler obstacles resolved
eﬃciently by local adaptation while complex scenarios trigger comprehensive
replanning.
Key Findings. The multi-level RL architecture provides measurable path quality
improvements (4.5–5.2% reduction in path length) at the cost of moderate computational
overhead (approximately 55% increase in computation time). Given that computation times
remain suitable for real-time operations and the path eﬃciency gains directly translate to
reduced battery consumption and faster mission completion, the multi-level architecture
represents a valuable contribution to the hybrid path planning system. The ablation conﬁrms
that the hierarchical structure provides beneﬁts beyond what a single-agent approach can
achieve.

Conclusion and Future Work

Summary of Contributions
The research presents MR-QLearning which represents an improved Q-learning system

that brings four essential advancements to reinforcement learning path-ﬁnding methods. The
research introduces four essential contributions which include an Experience Replay Buﬀer
adapted for heuristic-guided tabular Q-learning to enhance learning performance through
sample reuse and Uncertainty Quantiﬁcation that develops a new multi-factor conﬁdence
measure for uncertainty-based decision systems and an Adaptive Conﬁdence Threshold which
stands as the initial adaptive threshold system for RL-A* hybrid pathﬁnding that removes
the need for human adjustment and Transfer Learning which establishes an improvement for
grid-based RL pathﬁnding to allow environment knowledge transfer. The research introduces a
Hybrid A* + RL system that uses ﬁve RL improvements to merge traditional pathﬁnding with
reinforcement learning methods. The system uses RL-Guided Heuristic Learning to generate
adaptive heuristics which derive from learned Q-values during execution. The system performs
continuous learning during execution to update Q-values in real-time for achieving online
adaptation. The system uses RL-Based Path Reﬁnement to enhance A* paths through learned
preference information. The system uses Multi-Level RL Policies to create diﬀerent planning
agents which operate at various time scales. The system uses Obstacle Prediction to perform
anticipatory avoidance through learned understanding of object movements. The system unites
battery-aware routing with adaptive energy management and adaptive navigation strategies
which use terrain diﬃculty analysis and obstacle density awareness to create a full system
which fulﬁlls all three research targets.

6.2

Limitations and Constraints
The system shows excellent results in all tested scenarios yet need to address speciﬁc

restrictions that aﬀect its operation. The system needs an initial training period before it can
be deployed for operation but this training duration becomes a time limitation when operating

in uncharted environments without existing reference points. The obstacle prediction system
functions only when obstacles follow predictable movement patterns but it fails to predict
obstacles which move unpredictably thus restricting its proactive avoidance function to speciﬁc
dynamic situations. The system currently runs in environments that use discrete grid-based
structures but it needs fundamental changes to support continuous state and action spaces. The
fourth point involves primary evaluation of static barriers which performed restricted dynamic
obstacle assessment but complete evaluation with multiple moving obstacles would enhance
the veriﬁcation of dynamic avoidance functions. The dynamic environment evaluation on three
methods which included A* and D* Lite and the proposed Hybrid approach because of limited
time available during the research period. The evaluation of dynamic environments needs to
include Double Q-Learning and MR-QLearning and Neural A* for complete assessment of
all methods operating in environments that has static and dynamic obstacles.The experimental
evaluation, while comprehensive, operates within certain boundary conditions that limit the
generalizability of the ﬁndings. The 2D grid-based simulation environment abstracts several
aspects of real-world drone navigation, including three-dimensional ﬂight dynamics, sensor
noise, and communication latency. The obstacle prediction mechanism demonstrates high
eﬀectiveness for obstacles with consistent movement patterns but provides no beneﬁt for
truly random obstacle motion. In real-world scenarios where obstacle behavior may be more
complex, the prediction component would need enhancement. The current implementation
assumes perfect state observation, with the drone having complete knowledge of its position and
the positions of all obstacles within its sensing range. Sensor noise and localization uncertainty
in real deployments would introduce additional challenges.
6.2.1

Future Research Directions
The research has multiple potential paths which scientists can use to advance their work.

The algorithms need expansion to operate with continuous state and action spaces which
would enable their use in realistic navigation tasks that produce smooth paths. The system
would achieve better aerial drone navigation through three-dimensional environment extension
which would allow it to plan altitudes and detect obstacles in three-dimensional space. The

agents and performing joint tasks. The evaluation of physical drone platforms in real-world
conditions would determine their actual operational capabilities through tests of sensor systems
and communication delays and natural environment challenges. The system would beneﬁt from
deep reinforcement learning integration because it would enable the handling of complex

move without warning and change direction because it switches to defensive mode which uses
prediction sets from the prediction component. The prediction system needs to evolve into a

prediction algorithms.

Conclusion
The research develops an autonomous drone path planning system which fulﬁlls three

and battery-aware routing for energy optimization and adaptive navigation for complex
urban environments. The research shows that hybrid systems which unite A* pathﬁnding
with reinforcement learning produce better results than standalone methods because they
oﬀer ﬂexible performance that classical and reinforcement learning systems cannot achieve
independently. The research shows A* provides optimal solutions but reinforcement learning
enables both learning and adaptive capabilities. The MR-QLearning algorithm establishes
new standards for reinforcement learning pathﬁnding through its four original contributions
while the Hybrid A* + RL system proves successful pathﬁnding through its ﬁve additional
improvements. The system delivers immediate replanning functions which fulﬁll the need for

a base for upcoming studies which will expand to continuous areas and three-dimensional
domains and multiple agents and actual system implementation. The expanding use of
